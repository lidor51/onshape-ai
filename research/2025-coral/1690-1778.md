# 1690 And 1778: 2025 Coral Intake Evidence

Research date: 2026-09-12. Season: 2025 REEFSCAPE. Team 1778 is literal; no substitute team or season.

Update 2026-09-18: this historical text-research pass is superseded only where
noted by [the actual native inspection record](CAD-INSPECTION.md). Selected 1690
source meshes and hierarchy have now been inspected, including a Y-up chassis
datum and slotted front contact. 1778 geometry access failed; 2056 withheld native
robot CAD. Earlier LINK-ONLY labels describe the September 12 pass, not current
1690 inspection status. Neither the source inspection nor sampled prototype-video
frames establish a completed, manufacturing-ready adapted subsystem.

## Findings

- Both nominations have verified first-party 2025 release threads: 1690's Whisper and 1778's SubZero. Their designation as "great intakes" remains the user's nomination, not an independently verified comparative-performance result.
- 1690's strongest evidence is Korny's dated, 14-iteration account: separating acquisition from orientation enabled progress, but simplifying away a rear roller introduced a real-robot dead spot (E03).
- 1778 documents pickup assisted by robot rotation, mechanical centering during intake raising, and handoff failures sensitive to lower-axle motion and receiver alignment (E07-E11).
- Actual released CAD links and team-posted pictures were located. No 2025 technical-binder URL was found for either team within this bounded search; do not invent one or equate a resource-page heading with a binder.

## 1690: Reported Behavior And Revisions

All mechanism observations in this table are AUTHOR-REPORTED, primarily E03's retrospective. They are not independent acquisition-rate or durability measurements.

| Topic/revision | Reported evidence | Limitation or lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pickup, 2025-01-06 to 01-08 | Initial concept tried to orient coral horizontally while lifting it over the bumper. Above about 60 degrees angle between coral and bumper, the first prototype could stand coral on its narrow end and jam. Moving-chassis tests and sideways driving helped but did not remove sticking (E03). | Early failure, not a limit measured on the final robot. Test moving acquisition, not only a stationary bench. |
| Architecture change, from 01-08 | Team separated flexible acquisition from an internal orienting indexer. It compared horizontal rollers against a wall with give versus a V-shaped wheel arrangement; the V approach was selected for orientation and centering (E03). | The receiving orientation influenced intake/elevator layout. This is evidence for jointly defining acquisition and handoff, not proof that every intake needs a V indexer. |
| Centering details, 01-09 onward | Moving the V-indexer wheels toward the coral edge corrected an initially disappointing test. Side slides were later removed because they did not help; the team retained a floating front roller and polycarbonate ramp (E03). | Do not confuse lateral slides with the eventual centering mechanism or preserve unsuccessful guide features just because an early picture shows them. |
| Roller contact, January | A compliant lower roller tested better than a hard one. Stars supplied useful deflection in limited space; E01 describes the bottom wheel axle becoming a vulcanized-rubber roller. Position, diameter, and compliance were iterated (E01/E03). | Evidence concerns contact behavior/compliance; no measured friction coefficient, durometer, roller RPM, torque, or compression is provided. Supplier equivalence is UNKNOWN. |
| Simplification and failure, 01-15 to 02-14 | Replacing the back roller with a static polycarbonate ceiling worked in a prototype. On the assembled robot a dead spot appeared; prototype momentum had masked it. A third rear roller was added on 02-14; the rough retrofit still hung up, while the competition intake built around it reportedly worked well (E03). | Validate the installed ramp/indexer transition at low momentum and with realistic support stiffness. Fewer rollers was not automatically better. |
| Later competition revision | After ISR event 3, the front roller was changed to stars only to improve lollipop acquisition. E05 describes three 5-inch intake rows and an aligning mix of 5-inch stars and 3-inch wheels, orienting coral parallel to the elevator (E03/E05). | Preserve revision differences; these are reported nominal sizes, not measured geometry or a complete roller BOM. |
| Extension and impact response | Whisper used a collapsing four-bar, current limiting, relatively fast gearing, and surgical tubing assisting lift. Korny's edited explanation places the lower front pivot above the driven arm's main rotation axis so impact folds it upward (E04). | No exact link lengths, ratio, current limit, actuator selection, sweep, or impact load was established. His low-maintenance claim is local to this intake, not a whole-robot guarantee. |
| Transfer and jam cleanup | E03 targets a repeatable indexed position/orientation; E05 identifies the vacuum receiver. The rear-roller revision addresses a transfer dead spot. | Exact receiver pose, transfer tolerance/timing, reverse/eject sequence, trapped-coral removal access, and jam-clearing success remain UNKNOWN. |

E02 reports intake development continuing through weeks 5-7 and smaller competition-season changes. E01's rough estimate of about ten iterations is superseded in detail by E03's fourteen labeled iterations, not treated as an exact count conflict.
Adjacent integration warning: E12 reports two complete belt-splice delaminations, replacements for wear, and an emergency reinforcement during Einstein; the author advises against copying that elevator splice. Successful competition operation did not establish a reliable receiver transmission.

## 1778: Reported Behavior And Revisions

E10 is a first-person account with a less explicit role attribution; the stationary-axle finding is independently corroborated within the team posts E07/E08. Do not promote unrelated forum replies into 1778 facts.

| Topic | Reported evidence | Limitation or lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pickup orientation | Kelly says perpendicular coral can be gripped and then falls into the intake/centering path as the robot rotates; the drive team learned to handle this without redesign (E10 post 44). | Driver-assisted recovery, not demonstrated orientation-independent pickup. No angular envelope, attempt count, cycle-time penalty, or lollipop coverage established. |
| Centering and raising | ApolloG says coral detection automatically raises the intake and starts handoff; coral is centered by the time it is up (E09). July clarification says the single linebreak detects possession, not centering (E11 post 52). | Presence and correct transfer pose are distinct. The reported expected centering outcome is not a measured guarantee or an extra alignment sensor. |
| Lower axle and handoff | Originally free-spinning lower wheels allowed coral to rotate in place instead of ejecting properly. The team immobilized the axle with Gorilla tape; ApolloG reports improved handoff and trough scoring, and peble says the fixed axle was essential (E07/E08/E10). | This is an intentional stationary contact condition, not simply a higher-grip powered roller. A reproduction must specify which surfaces rotate. |
| Contact-wheel change | Kelly describes a separate wheel receiving tape to help lift/push coral out, and suggests relocating that wheel if redesigning the plates (E10 post 47). | Do not conflate this tape with axle-locking tape. Contributions of friction versus effective contact geometry are UNKNOWN; plate relocation was a suggestion, not a verified later modification. |
| Failed transfers | Kelly suspects mispositioning behind thrown coral and reports matches with an incorrectly centered arm; depth effects were explicitly uncertain (E10 post 47). ApolloG describes tuning manipulator voltage so current sensing detects a stalled, gripping coral (E11 post 50). | Keep the causal account qualified. Intake centering alone does not guarantee receiver alignment; no transferable current threshold or voltage was supplied. |
| Motors and maintenance | February layout post reports two X60s and a possible future change to X44s for weight. Eli Winckler's (`peble`) sensor-mounting account describes Adafruit linebreaks as awkward and proposes LaserCANs for future robots (E07/E08). | Neither proposed substitution is evidence it happened or that a specific contemporary part is compatible or legal. |
| Extension and jam cleanup | Intake raising is part of the transfer sequence; fixes address in-place spinning and poor ejection (E07-E11). | Exact deployment linkage, reach, stops, collision compliance, jam timeout/reverse logic, removal access, and recovery time remain UNKNOWN. Do not import 1690's four-bar into 1778's description. |

## Gaps And Evidence Gate

- Geometry UNKNOWN: roller center distances, compression, guide profiles, link lengths, true deployment envelope, receiver tolerances, shaft fits and exact configurations. No authorized CAD file was measured; nominal reported sizes are not a reconstruction.
- Test hypotheses, not work executed: compare angle/offset/upright pickups on a moving chassis; check low-momentum dead spots; deliberately offset the receiver; distinguish possession from alignment; verify controlled ejection, reversal, jam access, and re-deployment after impact.
- No usable 2025 binder found for either team; no PDF pages can be cited. Source existence outside the sampled pages remains open. CAD-link discovery is complete for this step; CAD content validation is not.
- No measured success rates, friction/compliance data, load/torque/current sizing, material qualification, vendor availability audit, or official 2025 rules review. Those remain prerequisites before mechanism selection or CAD implementation.
- No whole-thread or edit-history completeness claim: 1778's middle release window was not fetched, and later replies elsewhere may qualify these accounts. Kelly's exact team role remains unverified. No source was substituted for literal 1778 or silently carried across seasons.

## Findings

- **Verified, author-reported relationship:** 6328's Dave Powers says its March ground-intake development was "inspired mostly by 2056," with additional influence from 1690, 1678 and 2481 [S8, post 439]. This supports learning from 2056, not a dimensionally identical copy. His October post separately identifies a 2056-inspired offseason rebuild, 25O [S2].
- **2056's correction is directly relevant to handoff:** Khalsa reports that the original mechanically coupled straightenator sides could jam when coral entered parallel to the intake. The revision independently powered the two sides; geometry did not materially change [S9, post 120].
- **6328 separates pickup from orientation/buffering:** its prototypes progressed from floor acquisition to an indexer that finishes orientation and holds coral while the end effector is away from its loading pose [S8, post 439].
- **Prototype success did not establish competition robustness:** after Champs, Powers reported an insufficiently robust intake and reduced elevator rigidity after removing the hopper [S10]. Treat the March prototype videos as development evidence, not a reliability qualification.

## Relevance To This Team

The following are **inferences or research directions**, not statements that either reference robot matches the user's manufacturing resources.

- **Router CNC / flat aluminum and polycarbonate:** investigate plate-supported rollers and replaceable guides, with datums located by cut/machined parts. S10 warns that removing an apparently separate hopper can remove structural support; the standalone intake and receiver placeholder must each have an explicit load path. Do not depend on precision-bent brackets.
- **Manual lathe and mill:** S4 supplies a concrete example of turned pivot plugs, but the user's fit, retention and inspection process still needs engineering. Neither adhesive-only retention nor a pin is selected here.
- **Metric-first / WCP:** keep new plate/tube dimensions metric while preserving native COTS shaft, bearing, pitch and fastener standards. S8 post 436 mentions substituting small metric pulley bearings, but does not establish an all-metric robot or the bearing part numbers. Verify vendor fits; do not round imperial interfaces into nearby metric sizes.
- **Kraken X60 / X44:** S9 gives an explicit X44 reference for each straightenator side. It does not size our intake, pivot or receiver motor, nor justify transplanting 3:1. Compare torque, surface speed, current and packaging after tests define loads. X60 use here remains a preference, not a source finding.
- **In-house gearboxes, MAXPlanetary, ThriftyBot, selective REV ION:** evaluate only once a transmission is needed; motor compatibility, ratios and loads are unverified. WCP source/version and manufacturer part numbers must be checked before selecting catalog parts through FRCDesignLib.
- **Contact parts / printing:** compare AndyMark compliant wheels with rubber-covered tube through physical tests. The reviewed evidence does not establish either team's exact contact material or friction. Prusa MK4 guides/spacers are candidates; confirm the other printer model/materials before assigning structural duties.

## Handoff Questions And Tests

**Working hypothesis H1 (inference):** separating acquisition from independently controllable singulation/buffering may improve transfer reliability when entry is skewed or the receiver is unavailable. S8 motivates the stages; S9 motivates testing drive coupling. Neither proves the best layout for this team.

Keep both a fixed acquisition arrangement and a deployable/pivoting arrangement open. Fixed versus floating front contact is a separate question from whether the entire intake deploys. Do not choose the final design from these references alone.

| Interface owner | Rough interface / unanswered question | Discriminating check before detailed CAD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chassis | Define datum, mounting pattern, bumper boundary, allocated volume and loads on a blank chassis. | Envelope sketch plus load-path review; ensure removing hopper/intake does not remove receiver support, prompted by S10. |
| Intake | Which coral entry orientations, speeds and offsets must be accepted? How much front-roller compliance is needed? | Matched fixed/floating contact trials, including perpendicular/parallel/skewed entry; count misses and record catches, prompted by S8. |
| Indexer / intake | What exit position, orientation and allowable error does the receiver need? Can coral wait safely without continual drive? | Buffer with receiver absent, then transfer across deliberate pose offsets. Compare coupled and independently driven alignment with the same geometry, prompted by S9. |
| Receiver placeholder | Who owns loading pose, clearances and ready/accepted signals? | Block/line envelope and state diagram for acquisition, waiting, transfer and withdrawal; test absent/late receiver and failed acceptance. |
| Controls | How are capture, jam, transfer complete and timeout distinguished? | Log current and sensors; inject a jam and distinguish it from normal pickup load. Verify safe reversal/driver abort rather than assuming S9's unspecified thresholds. |
| Shared structure / maintenance | Stowed/deployed sweeps, stops, cable travel, guarding and removal access? | Clearance review in every state; repeat transfer under representative frame deflection and after service/reassembly. No dimensions assigned yet. |

Choose sample counts and acceptance limits with the user before testing. H1 is weakened if independent control does not reduce transfer failures under matched conditions, or adds unacceptable complexity/power. Official 2025 rules, loads, motor sizing, compression, contact life and exact tolerances remain separate unverified work.

Arm verdict: levels 1-2 + VERTICAL GOAL with a high-pivot arm dropping 4 cubes

Short answer: a single pin joint cannot carry a 4-cube magazine to GOAL 2 from a legal start. Three layouts that do work: a high shoulder plus a wrist (SF5: 34 x 26 in frame, drops of 4 at GOAL 2), one motor driving a four-bar (SF7: 30 x 28 in frame, 4 cubes indexed at GOAL 2), and an inclined lift plus tilt for GOAL 3 (SF6).

  1. GOAL 1 is out for a 4-cube column. GOAL 1 stands in the opponent LAUNCH ZONE, so the robot must stay under 48 in there (G416). A 4-stack over the 18 in rim tops out at 1437 mm against 1219 mm, and even the empty 950 mm column reaches 1437 mm. Score GOAL 1 with a partner or a separate low mechanism.
  2. GOAL 2 fits 4. The stack top is at 1945 mm, 37 mm under 78 in. Above GOAL 3 only 1 cube height fits.
  3. A column bolted to a single-pivot arm cannot get there legally. With the stowed intake and drivebase in the way, no pivot inside the robot can even rotate the GOAL 2 drop pose into a legal start pose, on either frame (table). The column is vertical at only one arm angle, and that angle cannot be reached from a legal stow.
  4. A levelling linkage keeps it vertical but fails elsewhere: the load point would sit 67 mm above the carpet (under the bumper top), and an always-vertical column cannot tilt to shoot.
  5. Shoulder + wrist works: every solution has the shoulder at 1010-1020 mm, which is the 42 in start ceiling, just behind centre, with a 682-712 mm arm. So your high-pivot instinct is right: as high as possible. But the column also needs its own joint. With vertical loading, each cycle is a single shoulder swing (the wrist chain holds the column's angle), and the wrist only moves to stow and to tilt for a shot.
  6. GOAL 3 + VERTICAL GOAL: an inclined single-stage lift with a tilting tray works on the standard 30 x 28 in frame. At GOAL 3 both joints are on hard stops, which is where its precision comes from.

Is one rotational DOF enough for GOAL 2 + VERTICAL GOAL?

  1. A single pin joint: no. A pin moves the magazine rigidly, so the load pose and the GOAL 2 pose fix the only possible pivot. With the tray loading in line with the tunnel, that pivot is at (-164, 1255) mm, above the 42 in start ceiling. With curved-handoff loading (100 deg, 90 deg, 80 deg up from horizontal) the pivot falls inside the robot at 943-1027 mm, but no rotation about it gives a legal start pose. For the 4-cube column the pivot is outside the robot, or does not exist. The 34 x 26 in frame gives the same answer.
  2. One motor driving a four-bar: yes, this is SF7 Rocker Tray. With the tray as the coupler, a four-bar passes three exact poses (start, load, GOAL 2) and turns the tray on the way. On the 30 x 28 in frame, 117511 linkages run start, then load, then GOAL 2 in order, without a dead point and inside 78 in / 18 in; 559 of them keep the front half low enough to hang. The path passes rear-up poses between loading and GOAL 2, and at the zone spot one of them is a legal VERTICAL GOAL shot (-30.1% / +30.1% speed tolerance). Costs: the shot is flat (32 deg), there is no fender shot, GOAL 1 and GOAL 3 are out, and the tray turns fast near the start pose.

Can SF6 also do GOAL 2?

No. Its lift is inclined, so height and reach move together. At GOAL 2 height the tilt axle would need to be at x 83 mm, but the lift line is at x -71 mm there: 153 mm short with the tray level. Even with any tilt, the best controlled drop (cube bottom 10-150 mm above the rim) is 98 mm short of the THROAT centre, and the THROAT allows about 25 mm. GOAL 2 would need a reach DOF (a drawer, as in SF3) or a different linkage (SF7 gets GOAL 2 but gives up GOAL 3).

Method: 2D side-view envelopes of the magazine only (column 950 x 218 mm, tray hull 904 x 324 mm), checked against 42 in and inside the frame at the start, clear of the drivebase (120 mm) and the stowed intake, and against 78 in, 18 in and the floor in motion. SF5 motion is searched on a joint grid (shoulder 2 deg, column 5 deg, wrist rate limited) with 30 mm vertical spare. SF6 paths are linear in carriage position and tilt. SF7 linkages are synthesised from three exact poses and driven in 90 steps on one assembly branch. Links, arm tubes, drives, wiring, dynamics and structure are not checked. Source: feasibility.json.

Game analysis: what makes a robot dominant when it is not executed perfectly

Cube supply is finiteEach alliance has about 61 cubes: 32 on its half, 26 in reserve and 3 preloads. Only the 14 centre-line cubes and the 4 beside IP-C are really contested. Elite alliances run out of cubes, so points per cube count, not just cycles.
Value of a full 4-cube tripGOAL 1: 8. GOAL 2: 12. VERTICAL GOAL: 16. GOAL 3: 20. The VERTICAL GOAL and GOAL 3 are both at the far end, so the trip is the same length.
Forgiveness is not equalThe hexagon is 30 in across the flats for a 9 in cube, giving about +/-266 mm of lateral margin. A THROAT is 11 in, giving +/-25 mm and +/-14.8 deg of yaw. There is only one GOAL 3, and a cube that jams in it blocks it (your simulator models a 12% jam chance per miss).
Reach budget at a HORIZONTAL GOALEvery goal backs onto a wall, so the throat centre is 12 in from the only face you can touch. With the bumper on that face the cube centre is 15.25 in outside the frame. That leaves 2.75 in (70 mm) of mechanism inside the 18 in limit: a placer needs a hard-stopped reach, not a long arm.
Height ladder42 in at the start, 32 in under the boards, 48 in inside the opponent LAUNCH ZONE, 78 in maximum. A steep shot whose underside is above 48 in by the time it clears your bumper cannot be touched by a legal defender.
Endgame is cheapThe rail top is at 33.8 in, so a robot only has to lift about 2 in to get off the carpet. A robot under 30 in can straddle the rail with its centre of mass underneath. HANG is worth 10 points, the same as 2.5 VERTICAL GOAL cubes.

Design principles used for every concept

  • Choose big targets: the VERTICAL GOAL before horizontal throats.
  • Let contact do the aiming: bumper on a field face (blue GOAL 1 as a fender, goal faces as depth stops), a fork that straddles the 24 in goal base, drawers that run to a hard stop.
  • Carry 4 cubes for the far end and keep one piece path (one handoff per cycle).
  • Stay under 32 in when stowed where the architecture allows it: the under-board lane, 4 short-only cubes and UNDER as an endgame fallback.

Recommendation (hypothesis, not a result)

Capacities are now what each mock physically holds per trip: SF1, SF2, SF5, SF6 and SF7 carry 4; SF3 carries 3 and SF4 carries 2. The earlier runs gave SF3 and SF4 one cube more than their trays hold, and gave SF1 no reload time between its two pairs.

  1. Highest ceiling in your simulator: SF6 Ramp Lift (83% win / 4.53 RP elite, 73% win / 4.19 RP strong, 27% win / 2.75 RP average; LEVELS RP 100% at strong). It puts 4 cubes into the 5-point GOAL 3 from hard stops and also shoots the VERTICAL GOAL (-30.1% / +30.1% speed tolerance at the fender, -26.9% / +30.1% at the zone spot). At average execution it is level with SF3, so its lead depends on executing well. Its task times are my estimates, and the tray sandwich and trapdoor indexing are unproven: they are the first things to prototype.
  2. Most tolerant when execution slips: SF3 Gantry Tower, now with its real 3-cube tray (56% win / 3.80 RP strong, 30% win / 2.84 RP average; your Tower preset gets 23% win / 2.67 RP at average). The fork mainly protects the LEVELS RP (21% vs 5% without it at strong). Cost: 5 DOF, 7 state changes per cycle and a 70 mm reach margin.
  3. SF5 Column Arm (your arm idea, made legal): GOAL 2 in drops of 4 plus the VERTICAL GOAL (-30.1% / +30.1% fender, -23.0% / +30.0% zone) and HANG. Simulator: 52% win / 3.55 RP strong, 19% win / 2.49 RP average; LEVELS RP 95% at strong. Its costs are a 34 x 26 in frame, a long lever at 78 in, and no GOAL 1.
  4. SF7 Rocker Tray (one motor): GOAL 2 four cubes per trip plus zone-spot VERTICAL GOAL shots (-30.1% / +30.1%) and HANG, on the standard frame. Simulator: 42% win / 3.25 RP strong, 20% win / 2.51 RP average; LEVELS RP 96% at strong. Fewest positioning DOF of the placer-shooters, but a flat shot and no fender shot.
  5. SF1 Brass Cannon with honest 4-cube indexing (two pairs, one barrel reset): 40% win / 3.22 RP strong, 10% win / 1.74 RP average, against your Launcher preset's 50% win / 3.55 RP / 20% win / 2.00 RP. It stays the best under-board and UNDER option, and its release clears a legal defender (-13.6% / +13.8% fender, -3.7% / +6.1% (lane L) and -5.4% / +7.7% (lane R) zone). Your simulator applies the same accuracy penalty to the hexagon as to the THROATs, so it cannot credit the bigger target; shooter-only robots look worst when execution slips.
  6. Budget floor: SF2 Mortar Rider (18% win / 2.32 RP strong, 10% win / 1.57 RP average): fewest positioning DOF, but it is only reliable from the fender and cannot hang.
  7. SF4 Forge Hybrid is the complexity ceiling with only 2 cubes per trip (18% win / 2.50 RP strong, 8% win / 2.12 RP average). Not recommended.

Next discriminating tests, cheapest first: (1) a cube shooter test rig at the fender and at 1.6 m (speed band, tumble, whether the cube passes above 48 in at the bumper line); (2) the 2-lane intake wedge with cubes arriving centred; (3) a cardboard goal fork and trapdoor tray on a real 24 in goal box, and the SF6 tray sandwich at 50 deg; (4) the rail hook and 2 in lift, with measured centre-of-mass offset.

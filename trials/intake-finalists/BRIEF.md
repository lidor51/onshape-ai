## Controlling Requirements

- The robot uses holonomic swerve. Side entry has no inherent translation or
  turn-time penalty. Compare actual packaging, visibility, piece orientation and
  complete field cycles, not a fictional need to turn before strafing.
- Interpret 07 as a mounting/routing architecture where appropriate; do not discard
  it solely because it is on the side. Front/side transformations must preserve
  mechanism lengths and relative clearances. Chassis dimensions can limit packaging.
- Aim for a simple, high-value mechanism appropriate to an elite alliance role.
  Minimize unnecessary handoffs, driven axes and recovery sensitivity, not capability
  indiscriminately. No invented speed, friction, success rate or load validation.
- One nominal coral: L301.625, OD114.3, bore101.6 mm. Check the full body, not just
  its center. Approximate piece variation/compression must be separate assumptions.
- Common chassis x=-350..350, y=0..760, z floor. Intact reference bumper outer
  x=+/-435, y=-85..845, z45..165. Extension <=457.2 mm from ROBOT PERIMETER;
  start height <=1066.8 mm. No intake-size bumper gap. Receiver geometry is ours to
  propose, but its axes, ownership and open/closed access must be explicit.
- Shop: router-cut aluminum/polycarbonate, manual mill/lathe, printing; no accurate
  metal bends. Metric custom geometry, exact imperial purchased interfaces later.

## Geometry-Ready Definition

Required before presenting a finalist as passing this **bounded** gate:
1. A parameterized physical assembly with constant part lengths and real motion
   axes or explicitly constrained slides; no independently teleporting snapshot arms.
2. Identified acquisition, capture, retained transfer, stow, handoff and return states.
   The full coral moves with its retaining mechanism or along an explicit contact path.
3. Stated pickup-orientation envelope. Screen the important yaw/offset cases; do not
   claim all-angle acquisition when only a centered crosswise path has been checked.
4. Actual bumper/frame clearance, positive-margin hardware extension/stow checks,
   and receiver/tool clearance across the motion, not only endpoint center checks.
5. Explicit powered/passive contacts, potential dead spots, geometric retention,
   and release ownership. Geometry can establish possible contact, not traction or
   passive self-centering success. Physical contact/preload tests remain required.
6. A reproducible dimensioned data packet, checks with measured results, and clear
   open-versus-stowed/handoff views plus 2D path/flow. Targeted simple STEP solids are
   useful if available cheaply; avoid repeated vendor STEP imports or hardware detail.

If a required gate cannot be met, record FAIL/UNRESOLVED and repair the local cause
or reject that architecture. Do not rename unresolved cartoon drawings geometry-ready.
No whole-robot kinematics, field scoring, physics simulation or physical build is
authorized merely by this brief. This is the standalone intake and receiving interface.
